The DATE paper, section III-A, already observes that early gates have restricted populations. Its DITT encoding reasons about possible populations, and TCJAP conditions them on local gate values. The existing project certifies the four-input table using explicit signal sets and signed permutations. Its table for all signals and images is impractical with five inputs. We reuse truth-based gate generation and the independently computed four-input table. Percy implements topology-based SAT exact synthesis, and published fanout-free enumeration covers the tree case. Neither gate-count feasibility nor a free-standing read-once enumerator suffices to certify a cost cutoff against arbitrary reconvergent DAGs. The candidate claim concerns when the *actual paid frontier* of an unrestricted DAG forces all remaining gates to have disjoint support. Generic support counting, truth normalization, and polynomial-time recognition of read-once functions are prior techniques.

## Derivation

For n inputs and N=2^n rows, let w(s)=2*(N/2^s)*(N-N/2^s). A nonconstant signal depending on at most s variables has both its population and its complement population divisible by N/2^s, so costs at least w(s). Build a partition of the n inputs by uniting all variables in each available signal's essential support. Initially each input is separate. A gate can join at most two blocks. Between two disjoint blocks a genuinely binary operation on two nonconstant signals depends on their entire union.

If k blocks remain, order their sizes in descending order and write a(j) for the sum of the largest j. After j cross-block merges a new merged block contains at most j+1 original blocks. Therefore the j-th merger costs at least w(a(j+1)). A full-support output requires k-1 mergers, at most one free. A lower bound on *additional paid* activity is B=sum over j=1..k-2 of w(a(j+1)), with an empty sum equal to zero. Any additional gate beyond k-1, counting the output, raises this lower bound by at least m=w(n): if the output does not merge, all k-1 mergers are paid, including one costing at least m.

For k>=2 and residual budget R<B+m, every feasible suffix has exactly k-1 gates and every one merges two components. Its contracted dependency graph is a binary tree: cutting at the prefix gives at least k distinct leaves and a connected binary circuit with k-1 gates has at most k distinct leaves. Equality leaves no room for a second use or an unconnected gate. Each block must therefore be supplied by one available signal with the entire block as its support. Each subsequent merge must combine two entire blocks. This condition can be used recursively within an otherwise unrestricted DAG search. Existing paid signals can share arbitrary logic.

Example: with five inputs and the two paid signals AND(a,b) and XOR(a,b), four components of sizes 2,1,1,1 remain. Two additional paid mergers cost at least 224+120=344. A bound using only the number of paid gates so far requires just one more paid gate at a floor of 120.
