# Exact area–activity frontiers for a punctured subcube

Discovery at (5,852) produced classes 0x7 and 0x7f at cost 790 with five gates, despite their four-gate read-once realizations. Stage 12 stays open. The tiny change in visited states (35 to 34) does not establish a solver speed advantage. The following predictions were written before opening the reserved five- and six-input outputs or running an independent tree optimizer.

## Testable statements

Write N=2^n, W(s)=2(N/2^s)(N-N/2^s), P(t)=sum of W(s) from s=2 through t, B=P(n-1), and m=W(n). A paid gate costs 2p(N-p) for p one-rows. The output gate is free. Size includes the output. Primary inputs and edge inversions are free. Gates may implement any binary Boolean function. Discard unreachable, duplicate and functionally trivial paid wires when minimizing cost and size.

For n=q+k, q,k>=2, take F=(AND of q literals) AND (OR of k further literals). These q+k variables are distinct. Its on-set is a subcube with one point deleted. The predicted complete Pareto frontier is

| gate count | least attainable activity at that size |
|---|---|
| n-1 | P(q)+P(k) |
| n | B+m |

All sizes above n are dominated. Along q=k increasing, the reduction relative to the *best* size-minimal design tends to 50%. The cost is the DATE uniform logical switching model with equally weighted internal nodes. Delay, fanout-dependent capacitance and glitches would change the problem.

More strongly, for every n>=4, exactly three full-support NPN classes reach B: an n-literal minterm, an (n-1)-literal minterm subtracted from a literal slice as the final operation (equivalently a half cube with one point deleted), and XOR of a literal with an (n-1)-literal minterm. The second distinct level is B+m, attained by exactly the n-3 punctured-subcube classes with k=2,...,n-2. Class membership includes all input permutations and input/output phase choices.

Registered checks: compare full held-out cost tables at (5,1050) and (6,3664) with a separate live-network enumeration; check every returned witness by truth rows; use a target-driven independent tree decomposition over *all* binary gates on every bipartition to price every indicated size-minimal circuit (n=4..8); compare both constructive endpoint circuits, including gates, with exact small tables. Test different phases, variable orders, the three boundary shapes and out-of-family controls. Implement a recognizer and circuit constructor operating on actual input truth tables; validate through the baseline's normal integration interface on four inputs and expose a standalone wider-input interface. Account for verification cost, code changes and closest prior low-power algebraic techniques. Retain negative computational evidence.

## Proof

1. A connected binary cone with i gates has at most i+1 different primary inputs: counting its 2i incoming edges, at least i-1 must come from other gates. A full-support realization requires n-1 gates, and at equality every primary and every nonoutput gate has one outgoing use. The circuit is a read-once tree. The i-th paid gate in topological order depends on at most i+1 distinct inputs. Its minority count is a nonzero multiple of N/2^min(n,i+1). Therefore any size g>=n-1 circuit costs at least B+(g-n+1)m. Removal of constant, projection or redundant pieces gives a live nontrivial circuit to which this bound applies.

2. A read-once realization of a monotone essential function can be phased so every gate and leaf is increasing. For any node, setting sibling signals to sensitizing values along its path exposes that node or its complement. This is possible independently at every level because the subtrees use disjoint variables. A restriction of an increasing function is increasing, so each exposed node has an increasing orientation. At each node the two oriented children are nonconstant, independently attainable and increasing. Monotonicity in both children forces an essential monotone binary operation, hence AND or OR. In that orientation the lowest common ancestor of two leaves gives their induced binary connective whenever fixing the other leaves keeps both essential. Two OR-block variables admit an OR restriction; each cross pair and each pair of AND-block variables admits an AND restriction. The lowest common ancestor of all OR-block leaves therefore has operation OR and contains no AND-block leaf (otherwise take an OR-block leaf in its opposite child). This entire pure subtree computes the OR; the other internal gates compute AND.

3. The k-leaf OR subtree, including its paid root, costs at least P(k) by the positional argument, achievable by a chain. Contract it to a signal Y of probability alpha=1-2^(-k). The remaining tree has q gates including its free output. Its i-th paid gate contains at most i+1 leaves in the contracted tree. A pure gate combining a AND-block variables has minority probability 2^(-a), with a<=i+1. A mixed one has probability alpha*2^(-a), with 1<=a<=i, so its minority probability is at least 2^(-(i+1)) and at most one half. Each of these q-1 paid gates costs at least W(i+1). A separate AND chain on q inputs followed by the free AND of the two chains attains P(q)+P(k) exactly.

4. For n>=4 the smallest possible positive increment above the position lower bound of a paid gate in an (n-1)-gate tree is greater than m. At position i its lower minority count t=N/2^(i+1) ranges from 2 to N/4. The next possible count is at least 2t; its cost difference is 2t(N-3t). Concavity gives a minimum at one of the endpoints: min(4(N-6), N^2/8)>2(N-1). Thus any tree at or below B+m must achieve B. At equality the last paid gate is a minterm up to phase on exactly n-1 variables, so all earlier paid gates are in its cone. The free output joins it with the missing literal. Combining these independent operands gives precisely the three floor classes enumerated above. They have distinct minority counts 1, N/2-1, N/2, and literal chains attain B for each. A punctured subcube with k=2,...,n-2 has minority count 2^k-1, excluding all three.

5. Form the q-literal enabling conjunction H, then continue the same chain by adjoining the k *false* OR-block literals to form a full minterm M. The free output H AND (NOT M) computes F. Every paid gate is a minterm on one more variable than its predecessor. Its cost is P(n)=B+m, attained with n gates. Part 1 excludes cheaper circuits with n or more gates. Parts 3 and 4 exclude cheaper trees and identify the exact size-minimal optimum.

6. Conversely any additional class at B+m must use n gates, so all its paid gates saturate the position bounds. The final paid gate is a full minterm; its cone uses all n inputs and all n-1 paid gates. At each earlier position, a minimum-minority signal on i+1 distinct variables is also a minterm up to phase. To grow this cone from size i to i+1 the most recent subcone must contain all earlier gates and acquire one fresh literal (otherwise the new cone could not involve i+1 variables with i gates). For i>=2 the minority minterm of the expanded cone implies the previous minority minterm, because that is the unique one-point phase of the previous gate on its support. The paid signals hence form nested cubes down to M. A new class must use M at the output. Its other operand, up to phase, is a literal containing M or one of these nested cubes H. Only three joint patterns occur. Up to complementation the nonconstant output is M, H or H minus M. Full support and exclusion of the floor leave precisely the asserted n-3 classes.

7. As r tends to infinity, P(r)/N^2 approaches 2 times the sum from s=2 to infinity of (2^(-s)-4^(-s)), or 5/6. Along q=k growing, P(q)+P(k) divided by N^2 tends to 5/3 while P(n)/N^2 tends to 5/6. This proves the limiting reduction for the specified family.
