**Hypothesis.** One cost-limited traversal can give different, valid optimality bounds for targets beyond its explored budget. Compute the least cost at which each possible free-root operand can cross the search boundary, then join that profile to target-dependent feasible partner activities. A conventional budget cutoff, an extra level of witnesses, or counting unused ends cannot by itself establish this stronger certificate. A decisive counterexample is any excluded cheaper DAG. A decisive negative result is that the extra bounds close no cases, or that direct deeper enumeration costs less for the same certificates.

## First-crossing argument

Let S contain the distinct normalized nontrivial paid signals already produced. Its price c is their activity sum. Positive prices and arbitrary fanout allow any optimum to omit repetitions, aliases, constants and dead gates. Signed input permutations act simultaneously on the entire state, preserving attainability and price. Enumerate every attainable state up to price K. On every first edge to a state T with price b>K, evaluate the free output using the new wire with each earlier wire. Free outputs independent of that wire are already represented earlier. Save witnesses and minimum prices U per target orbit. Multiple topological orders cannot invalidate a lower bound taken over all such crossings.

For each operand orbit i let H[i] be the least b of any crossed T that contains an operand of type i among its paid wires or primary inputs. Let b0 be the minimum crossed price. A completion that uses further paid gates has a free root using at least one later signal in a dead-free optimum. If the other operand has type i at the crossing, let M(i,f) be the minimum price of a normalized partner that can implement an orientation of target f with that operand and one library gate. It is safe to relax availability and identity. For a fixed orientation of f enumerate all normalized p in orbit i. For each of the five operators solve op(p,q)=f row by row with q(0)=0. The feasible q form a Boolean subcube. Its possible populations are an integer interval. The concave price takes its minimum at an endpoint. A paid q must be nonconstant, so intersect with [1,N-1]. Taking the minimum over p is equivalent to varying the target orientation at a fixed operand, since signed transformations preserve the normalized gate library. Output complement normalization is included.

If both root operands are later, their minority row sets have sizes x,y>=1. Outside their union both operands and hence the output are constant, so x+y>=a, the minority count of f (also true if the union covers all rows). Concavity and monotonicity on the minority range imply their total price is at least D(f)=w(1)+w(max(1,a-1)), where w(j)=2j(N-j). The operands are distinct in an optimum. Therefore unseen dead-free completions cost at least

    L(f) = min( b0 + D(f), min_i (H[i] + M(i,f)) ).

Every optimum was witnessed, or belongs to this unseen category. Thus min(U,L) is a global lower bound, and finite U<=L proves optimality. An empty boundary means exhaustion. H has one entry per orbit and can be reused across targets. We discard correlations between multiple boundary wires only in a relaxation. This establishes a numerical bound, not a proof of feasible witnesses. Revalidate circuits separately.
